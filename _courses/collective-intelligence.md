This course introduces the principles and methods of collective intelligence — how groups of simple, locally interacting agents produce robust, adaptive, intelligent group-level behavior. The course follows a single arc: the field map, the individual agent, the emergent collective, and engineering applications. Themes Six canonical domains are covered: collective motion (Vicsek, Reynolds boids), foraging and stigmergy (ant trails, ACO), division of labor (response thresholds), distributed decision-making (honeybee quorum, best-of-N), synchronization (Kuramoto), and opinion dynamics (DeGroot, Hegselmann–Krause). Concepts Across domains, students develop a unifying toolkit: the agent–environment–interaction framework, local rules with limited sensing, positive and negative feedback, phase transitions and order parameters, mean-field analysis, network topology, and robustness under perturbation. Research and practice Collective intelligence is an active interdisciplinary field at the intersection of computer science, statistical physics, biology, and the social sciences. The course connects these foundations to the engineered systems where they now operate — swarm robotics, federated and multi-agent machine learning, distributed consensus and blockchain protocols, multi-agent LLM systems, and crowd computing — and teaches students to recognize the collective-intelligence structure underlying them. Outcome Students leave with a working vocabulary for any collective system, hands-on experience implementing and analyzing an agent-based model, and the ability to formulate, measure, and communicate a collective-intelligence research question — transferable to research and to the design of modern decentralized systems.
