Our research focuses on developing Brain–Robot Interfaces (BRIs) that enable people to interact with and control robots directly through brain signals. By combining electroencephalography (EEG) with advanced AI models, we decode users’ brain activity to translate their intentions into meaningful robotic actions. Beyond direct brain-based control, we also develop intelligent neurofeedback systems that use EEG to continuously monitor users’ cognitive and mental states, such as attention, workload, and engagement. This information can be used to adapt the robot’s behavior and interaction to the individual user, with the goal of creating more personalized, intuitive, and responsive human–robot interactions.
